# A2 · Unidad 1: Conocer y conocerse

A textbook-led study sample for ¡Vamos! Español. Three core sessions, approximately
20 minutes each, plus short vocabulary sessions and later recall practice. The
three core sessions alone do not cover the entire vocabulary requirement.
This is authored lesson content for review, not a deployed app
feature. Explanations, practice sentences and role-plays below are original;
textbook activities and recordings remain linked to their sources.

**Your goal:** introduce yourself, find shared interests, explain what you find
difficult and recommend a useful activity to another learner.

### A small learning set at a time

Introduce 6–8 new entries per vocabulary session, with fewer if recall is
difficult. This is a pacing choice, not a claim that the entire glossary fits
into three lessons. Continue through each group's remaining entries and bring
previously studied words back in later sessions.

For each entry:

1. **Understand:** Spanish form, English meaning, noun article or adjective
   variation where relevant, and one short contextual example.
2. **Recognize:** identify it in a sentence or recording. Use textbook audio
   where the word actually occurs; use clearly labelled authored examples for
   glossary items absent from those recordings.
3. **Recall:** produce the Spanish word or phrase from a meaning or situation,
   initially with a hint if needed, then without one.
4. **Use:** say or write a new sentence using the current grammar. Carry a few
   target expressions into the next dialogue and writing task.
5. **Return:** check recall on a later visit. A revealed answer or a single
   successful multiple-choice attempt does not establish retained knowledge.

Expand the glossary's abbreviated combinations into complete learning entries:
*hacer deporte, hacer planes, hacer un intercambio, hacer yoga*; *ver películas,
ver series*; *tener curiosidad, tener dudas, tener fluidez*. Preserve reflexive
forms and prepositions such as *ponerse en forma* and *apuntarse a un evento*.
An expression repeated in two categories can share a learning record while
remaining visible under both textbook categories.

### Example integrated vocabulary set

Target set: **sociable, tímido/a, hacer amigos, hacer un intercambio,
conocer gente, el perfil, apuntarse a un evento, tener dudas**.

- Teach: meanings and forms, then notice the relationship between being shy,
  meeting people and choosing a language exchange.
- Recall: “sign up for an event” → *apuntarse a un evento*. Practise the full
  expression, including its preposition.
- Grammar: *Me cuesta hacer amigos. Me interesa hacer un intercambio.*
- Listening: use track 1 to identify how people find groups and events; do not
  assume all eight target expressions occur in that recording.
- Dialogue: **Soy un poco tímida y quiero conocer gente. ¿Qué me recomiendas?**
  Answer with a relevant recommendation, then ask one follow-up question.
- Writing: draft a short profile using at least four of the set naturally.
  Example: *Soy sociable y quiero hacer un intercambio para conocer gente.
  Me interesa apuntarme a un evento.*
- Later recall: explain what you would do to meet people, with the word list
  hidden. Revisit the expressions that needed help.

Track **not introduced**, **learning**, **recalled without a hint**, and
**recalled again on a later visit** separately for each entry. Keep vocabulary
coverage separate from activity completion; report actual recall evidence, not
an automatic CEFR advancement. All ten glossary groups remain required even
when the learner chooses a personal theme for dialogue.

## Session 1 · This is me

### Learn from the book

Open printed pp. 8–10. Look at the personality activity and the leisure-word
combinations. Choose two personality words and three activities that describe you.
Then read the profiles on p. 11: notice how a person connects personality with
interests and difficulties.

Start with these eight items from the required glossary; choose which to use in
your introduction. The remaining glossary entries stay on your learning checklist:

| Spanish | Meaning | Original example |
|---|---|---|
| tranquilo/a | calm | Soy tranquilo, pero me gusta conocer gente. |
| tímido/a | shy | Soy un poco tímida cuando hablo español. |
| sociable | sociable | Mi compañero es muy sociable. |
| casero/a | someone who likes staying at home | Soy casera y me encanta leer. |
| quedar con amigos | meet up with friends | Me gusta quedar con amigos los sábados. |
| hacer deporte | do sport / exercise | Me encanta hacer deporte al aire libre. |
| ver series | watch series | Prefiero ver series en español. |
| ir de compras | go shopping | No me gusta mucho ir de compras. |

**Notice the grammar.** In *me gusta*, the thing or activity determines the verb:

- One thing: **Me gusta el cine.**
- An activity: **Me gusta leer.**
- Several things: **Me gustan los libros.**

*Encantar* and *interesar* follow the same pattern: *Me encanta bailar; me
encantan los museos; me interesa la historia*. Change the pronoun for the person:
*me, te, le, nos, os, les*. For example: *A Ana le gustan los libros*.

To respond to someone's tastes:

| What they say | You agree | You disagree |
|---|---|---|
| Me gusta bailar. | A mí también. | A mí no. |
| No me gusta bailar. | A mí tampoco. | A mí sí. |

With *prefiero*, use *yo*: *Yo prefiero leer. —Yo también.*

### Practise, then use it

Complete before looking at the answers:

1. Me ___ las películas españolas. (gusta / gustan)
2. A Sara le ___ hacer deporte. (encanta / encantan)
3. Nos ___ los idiomas. (interesa / interesan)
4. Your partner says, “No me gusta ir de compras.” You agree: ___
5. Your partner says, “Me encanta bailar.” You disagree: ___
6. Without looking above, say “meet up with friends” in Spanish.

Answers: 1 **gustan**; 2 **encanta**; 3 **interesan**; 4 **A mí tampoco**;
5 **A mí no**; 6 **quedar con amigos**.
If you miss 1–3, identify whether the subject is a plural noun or an infinitive,
then try: *Me ___ aprender idiomas* (**gusta**).

### Speak · Meet your study partner

First practise this original reference sentence using the Claro loop:

> Soy un poco tímido, pero me gusta conocer gente y me encantan los idiomas.
>
> I'm a little shy, but I like meeting people and I love languages.

Read → record → hear yourself → review one or two suggestions → hear the
reference → retry. Use *tímida* if appropriate. A recognized-word mismatch is a
reason to listen again, not a confirmed pronunciation error.

Then answer with your own information, not the reference sentence:

- Partner: **¿Cómo eres y qué te gusta hacer?**
- Partner, after your answer: **A mí me gusta leer. ¿Y a ti?**
- Partner: **No me gusta ir de compras. ¿Y a ti?**
- Finish: tell the partner one interest you share, or one difference.

Support if needed: *Soy… Me gusta… Me encantan… A mí también/tampoco…*
The partner should accept honest disagreement and react to what you actually say.

### Write · Your introduction

Write 4–5 sentences: one personality description, two interests, one preference
and a question for your partner. Try one singular and one plural form of *gustar*
or *encantar*. Example opening: *Hola, soy Alex. Soy una persona tranquila…*

Review: Did you introduce yourself? Did the verb match the thing you like?
Keep your first draft, review advisory corrections, and save your revision.

## Session 2 · Find people with shared interests

### Recall, then learn

Without the vocabulary list, say two things you like and one you dislike.
Return difficult expressions to your review list.

Open printed p. 11. Add these useful phrases:

- **tener intereses en común** — have shared interests
- **conocer gente** — meet people
- **apuntarse a una actividad** — sign up for an activity
- **hacer amigos** — make friends

Use **me cuesta + infinitive/singular noun** and **me cuestan + plural noun**:
*Me cuesta hablar en grupo. Me cuesta la pronunciación. Me cuestan las
conversaciones rápidas.* This describes difficulty, not dislike.

Try: *Me ___ recordar palabras nuevas* (**cuesta**); *Me ___ los horarios*
(**cuestan**). Then finish *A mí me cuesta…* truthfully.

### Listen · The textbook's real audio

Play student-book **PISTA 01**, accompanying p. 11.

1. First listen: what problem do these applications help people solve?
2. Second listen: which application is described as connecting sports players?
3. Which one brings together groups with shared interests such as languages?
4. Which one has events created by its users and, according to the recording,
   operates only in Spain?

Check after listening: 1 meeting people/finding people to share activities with;
2 **Timpik**; 3 **Meetup**; 4 **GeoKeda**.
If needed, open the transcript on printed p. 155, find the relevant section,
then listen again with the transcript hidden. No invented clip timestamps are
required; use the full track until accurate segments have been mapped.

### Speak · Choose a group together

Role-play: you and a new classmate want to find people with shared interests.

- Partner: **Me interesa hacer deporte. ¿Qué te interesa a ti?**
- You: give a real interest and ask about the partner's interests.
- Partner: **Me cuesta hablar con gente nueva. ¿Y a ti?**
- You: describe a difficulty and suggest a type of group to join.
- Together: name one shared activity, or suggest an alternative if your tastes differ.

Retry sentence: **Me interesan los idiomas, pero me cuesta hablar en grupo.**
English: *I'm interested in languages, but I find it difficult to speak in a group.*

### Write · A short profile

Write 50–70 words for an imaginary language-exchange group. Include your
personality, two interests, one difficulty and what you hope to practise together.
Use *me gustan* and *me cuesta* at least once. The length is a guide, not a score.

Mini spelling check: **tu** means “your”; **tú** means “you”. Complete:
*¿___ quieres practicar con ___ compañero?* → **Tú / tu**.
Extend later with *mi/mí, el/él, si/sí, se/sé* from printed p. 111.

## Session 3 · Help each other learn

### Learn · Match an activity to a need

Open the activity programme on printed p. 12 and the needs on p. 13. Choose one
activity for someone who wants to practise speaking and another for someone
interested in art. Explain your choices; more than one can be reasonable.

Useful language: **el taller de pronunciación**, **la visita guiada**,
**recordar el vocabulario**, **entender a la gente**.

| Purpose | Pattern | Original example |
|---|---|---|
| Offer a possibility | Puedes + infinitive | Puedes escuchar un diálogo corto. |
| Make a personal recommendation | Te recomiendo + noun/infinitive | Te recomiendo practicar con una compañera. |
| Express a stronger need | Tienes que + infinitive | Tienes que reservar una plaza para participar. |
| Give general advice | Hay que + infinitive | Hay que practicar con frecuencia. |
| Explain what is useful or necessary | Es conveniente/necesario + infinitive | Es conveniente revisar las palabras nuevas. |

Complete: *Te recomiendo ___ un diálogo* (escuchar / escuchas) → **escuchar**.
Repair: *Hay que practicas* → **Hay que practicar**.
Then give your own recommendation for remembering vocabulary.

### Listen · Recommendations in context

Play student-book **PISTA 02**, accompanying p. 13. First identify the speakers
and the overall purpose. Then listen for these details:

1. What does Jake say he finds difficult?
2. Which activity does Raquel recommend to improve his accent?
3. What interests Vanessa, and which visit does Raquel recommend?
4. Which activity does Raquel recommend for meeting people and talking?

Answers: 1 speaking and understanding people; 2 the pronunciation workshop;
3 Spanish history/culture, the Reina Sofía museum; 4 café con Maca.
Check the transcript on printed p. 155 only after trying. Replay and notice a
recommendation phrase, then use its structure with your own words.

### Dialogue · Advise a new classmate

Partner: **Soy nuevo en la escuela. Me interesa la cultura, pero me cuesta
entender a la gente. ¿Qué me recomiendas?**

Your task: ask one question about the partner's interests, recommend a suitable
activity, and explain why. The partner asks one follow-up, such as **¿Y para
practicar la pronunciación?** Finish by explaining one of your own needs and
asking for a recommendation.

Support: *Si te interesa…, puedes… Te recomiendo… porque… A mí me cuesta…*
Reference sentence to record and retry:
**Si te cuesta recordar el vocabulario, puedes escribir frases sobre tu vida.**
English: *If you find it difficult to remember vocabulary, you can write sentences about your life.*

### Write · Your personal learning plan

Write 60–80 words to your new study partner. Include:

- an interest you share;
- one difficulty you have;
- two recommendations, connected to specific needs;
- one activity you want to do together.

Original model, shown only after a first attempt:

> Hola, Ana. A las dos nos interesan los idiomas y nos gusta el cine. A mí me
> cuesta entender las conversaciones rápidas. Para practicar, podemos escuchar
> un diálogo corto y comentarlo después. Si te cuesta recordar palabras, te
> recomiendo escribir frases sobre tu vida. También es conveniente revisar
> esas frases otro día. Me gustaría ver una película contigo y hablar de nuestros
> personajes favoritos. ¿Qué te parece?

## Finish and return

Adapt the book's final course-advice task on p. 14: save three recommendations
you actually want to follow. Make a short final introduction recording using
your own words; compare it with your first attempt by listening to both.

Self-check: Can I describe myself, respond to someone's tastes, explain a
difficulty and give a relevant recommendation? Mark each as *with help* or
*independently*. These are learner reflections, not certified proficiency levels.

On your next visit, recall five saved expressions, revisit missed grammar
questions in new sentences, and resume unfinished writing or dialogue. Show
concrete progress such as activities completed, words recalled and drafts revised.
Include required glossary entries in review even if the learner did not manually
save them. Show unintroduced entries and outstanding review separately so the
three core sessions cannot appear to complete the full vocabulary syllabus.

accounts. Do not infer mastery from time spent, recording quality or word matching.
